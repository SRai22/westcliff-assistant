Liu, F., He, X., Zhang, T., Chen, J., Li, Y., Yi, L., Zhang, H., Wu, G., & Shi, R. (2025). TickIt: Leveraging large language models for automated ticket escalation. Proceedings of the 33rd ACM International Conference on the Foundations of Software Engineering (FSE '25). https://doi.org/10.1145/3696630.3728558  
Summary: This paper introduces TickIt, a LLM-based framework, that is already deployed to production, for automating support ticket escalation within ByteDance’s Volcano Engine Cloud platform. Their previous static machine learning models treated escalation as a one-time binary classification problem. According to the authors, enterprise cloud support is dynamic and involves a high volume of activity, because of which the traditional methods don’t work very well.  The traditional models could not handle tickets that would be modified over time. As customers and support staff continue their conversation, there is more clarity and details, which might lead to change in context. But these models are unable to catch this because they treat each ticket as fixed. They also could not send tickets to the correct team based on the topic. 
The proposed solution, TickIt, solves these problems in the following ways: 
1. It keeps checking tickets as conversations happen in an online bypass mode and classifies them into predefined categories like system failure, customer complaint, asset loss, or others using a Chain-of-Thought prompted LLM. 
2. It avoids duplicate escalations by checking if a new issue is similar to one that was reported earlier. If it is, then the system combines them and rewrites the issue clearly to reduce confusion. 3. Category guided model fine-tuning: There is an active feedback loop. When the users and admins upvote or downvote decisions, the system uses that feedback to improve and retrain itself periodically through Supervised Fine-Tuning. 
The system during its deployment from August - December 2024, processed over 20,000 tickets and achieved an F1 score of 0.862. It also outperformed all the prompt-engineering-only baselines. During this time, TickIt reduced the mean time to repair (MTTR) by 39% and saved an estimated 10 person-days costs per day. 
Evaluation: This paper was published at ACM International Conference on the Foundations of Software Engineering (FSE '25), one of the top conferences in software engineering research. The authors are from ByteDance, which allows them access to production system data. The authors have evaluated SLM baselines, various prompt engineering configurations like Chain of Thought (CoT), In-Context Learning(ICL), Reflection, etc., and fine-tuning 5 family of language models. A limitation of the system mentioned in this paper is the domain specificity of the dataset. The tickets originated from this corporate cloud platform with well-defined structure, resources and lots of feedback from users specific to ByteDance. This context differs from this project’s setting, where the organization is a University, ticket volumes are lower, user language is less uniform, and feedback signals are difficult to collect. Additionally, the fine-tuning of the model is another investment which limits direct replicability of this paper. 
Application: TickIt’s architecture is a transferable design pattern for the AI Assisted Student Services System we are trying to build. The multi-class escalation framework of TickIt matches the 10-department routing problem that is addressed by this capstone project. Drawing inspiration from how the TickIt separates failure tickets and routes them to company on-call engineers while the complaint tickets are sent to customer managers, our system needs to correctly route financial aid tickets and academic advising tickets appropriately. 
This paper provided some important design considerations with data for this capstone. It showed that using a large language model with step-by-step reasoning performs slightly better than smaller embedding-based models, making it a better option for routing tickets. It also observed that fine-tuning the model with labeled data improved performance more than relying on prompt design.
Windiatmoko, Y., Rahmadi, R., & Hidayatullah, A. F. (2021). Developing Facebook chatbot based on deep learning using RASA framework for university enquiries. IOP Conference Series: Materials Science and Engineering, 1077(1), 012060. https://doi.org/10.1088/1757-899X/1077/1/012060 
Summary: This paper presented the design and implementation of a Facebook Messenger based Chatbot for handling student enquiries at Universitas Islam Indonesia. It was built using the open-source RASA framework with Long Short Term Memory (LSTM) Deep learning model. Students and prospective students frequently require information such as course schedule, grades, course syllabus, admission guidance etc. These services were affected by the COVID-19 pandemic. To solve this problem, they introduced a chatbot. This chatbot implemented two two modules: 1. A Natural Language Understanding module to handle intent classification using a supervised embedding classifier and named entity recognition through Conditional Random Field (CRF); 2. Dialog Management module to track conversation state and select the next best action using LSTM-based neural network. The chatbot had the capability to query information from a MySQL database and making REST Api calls to other sources on the internet. They selected Facebook Messenger as their deployment platform because of its higher reach in Indonesia. The performance of this model was measured and they found that the intent classifier had an F1-score of 0.98. 
Evaluation: 
This paper was published at the International Conference on Information Technology and Digital Applications in 2020. This is one of the top workshop-tier venues. This paper is a good implementation reference in the niche domain of university chatbots. The authors are affiliated with the Department of Informatics at Universitas Islam Indonesia, and this work is more a student-led project. The key limitations that can be observed here are: 
Lack of live user metrics like user satisfaction, task completion rate, handling edge cases. 
The data is limited to offline model metrics on a small dataset, so cannot claim generalization. 
There has been significant work in the technology since 2020, with RASA with a transformer based approach providing better performance. 
Application:  This paper is a direct domain precedent for our project. The academic categories this paper covers, for example course schedules, grade retrieval, admissions information, etc., have a good overlap with what our proposed system does. This paper  confirms that NLU and rule based systems can effectively handle university student services queries. 
From this work, it also becomes clear that pre-LLM approaches have limitations. This system has 14 hard-coded intent classes, and I strongly believe that it cannot accommodate the variations in language at a larger scale. If one has to add any new query into this model, it requires retraining the model on a new set of labeled data. This is where an LLM based system can perform better with dynamic natural language understanding that generalizes to new query types without retraining.  

Czechowski, N., Johnston, M., Kelty, E., & Zuñiga, F. (2024, October). 2025 EDUCAUSE top 10 #3: Smoothing the student journey. EDUCAUSE Review. https://er.educause.edu/articles/2024/10/2025-educause-top-10-3-smoothing-the-student-journey 
Summary: This article is an entry for issue #3 of the EDUCAUSE 2025 top 10, a leading non profit association for higher education technology professionals. The 2025 list is organized based on the theme of restoring trust in higher education, and this issue is titled “Smoothing the Student Journey” focusing on using technology and data to improve and personalize student services. 
The article documents how students must navigate a large number of institutional services, interactions, and systems in their academic life. They go from admission services, financial aid services, course registration, academic advising, etc. The fragmentation of these services across siloed departments creates confusion for students. The main idea of the article is that there should be minimum administrative burden on students, so that they can focus mainly on their educational goals. There is also this quote in the article: “ We're making sure that the end-to-end experience for a student who joins us is as slick as it can be. We're investing quite a lot of money in what we call the student journey. The market vision is from end to end, from first contact all the way through to becoming an alumni, they're in the system and we're not handing over from one department to another. We're not forgetting their information, we're not dropping it from one spreadsheet to another and retyping it, and all these kinds of things. So, they experience a continuous engagement with us, and they've not been forgotten about somewhere down the line.” The article discusses that institutions are pursuing strategies such as personalized student portals, campus-wide CRM platforms, ERP modernization(Workday Student), and AI-assisted services. The article offers 4 key recommendations for educational institutions: 
Involve students when designing the system. 
Take a holistic view of cross-organizational processes rather than optimizing in isolation. 
Start with small, achievable goals 
Avoid short-term cost-cutting. 
Evaluation :  EDUCAUSE Review is the flagship publication of EDUCAUSE, the leading professional association for higher education technology in the United States. 
The top 10 list is one of the most widely cited annual references in higher education fields. 
This was published in October 2024, for the 2025 planning cycle, making it directly relevant to this proposed solution. 
This article shows that the problem we are trying to solve with this project is a recognized, sector-wide concern identified by higher education leaders themselves, and not a localized issue. 
Application : This article serves as the primary institutional motivation source for this project. Many students face the issue of not knowing which department to contact for support, so their requests get delayed, getting routed to the wrong department, and circling around. EDUCAUSE highlights that this is a major issue in higher education for 2025, making organizational leaders invest more in this idea. This article also encourages the idea of using one centralized AI system instead of separate tools for each department. It's not that every department should have their own solution, the confusion would still exist. A centralized solution that is capable of receiving all student requests and routing them correctly removes the burden from students. This matches EDUCAUSE’s goal of creating a smooth and connected student experience. 

Razma, A., & Jurkus, R. (2026). AI-based classification of IT support requests in enterprise service management systems. Systems, 14(2), 223. https://doi.org/10.3390/systems14020223 
Summary: The paper mentioned here investigates automated classification of Information Technology Support Tickets in a real enterprise IT Service Management environment. Their main motivation was time cost and inconsistency of manual triage. Their dataset consisted of 150,441 tickets from a ITSM system at a Lithuanian food manufacturing company. First they reduced it to 87,859 after de-duplication preprocessing with TF-IDF cosine-similarity. These tickets had nine request types and eleven responsible teams. The tickets were mainly in Lithuanian language (70%), English (23%) and mixed-language content (7%). This makes the model to be necessarily multilingual. The authors propose a two-stage classification pipeline. Stage 1: Predict the request type. Stage 2: Predict the responsible team that handles the request. 
The two stages are evaluated from 2 perspectives: a diagnostic perspective that applied threshold-based random oversampling on the training dataset and deployment perspective that preserves the natural class imbalance. 
Evaluation: 
This paper was published in MDPI, a peer-reviewed open-access journal in February 2026. One of the most current empirical studies on AI-based ITSM classification. The authors are affiliated with the Marine Technology and Natural Sciences at Klaipeda University in Lithuania. The work is based on an enterprise production system with over 150,000 tickets, and the temporally disjoint 2025 holdout set, provides a good basis to believe that system is generalizable. 
Some of the limitations of the paper:  
The study is based on a single Lithuanian food manufacturing company, so direct application to other industries, countries, ITSM structures might be difficult. 
The oversampling strategy uses simple random duplication of minority-class examples rather than semantically aware augmentation. 
There is some discrepancy in stage 2 team assignment revaluation compared to stage 1 under the deployment perspective. 
Application: 
This paper is very similar to the current project. Both systems target multi-class text classification on an organization’s taxonomy, both operate on open-ended text input from users, and both route the requests to a fixed set of departments. The enterprise ITSM model with 9 request types and 11 teams is directly correlating with the 10 department structure of this Westcliff assistant project. Also, finding a macro-F1 score on a naturally imbalanced test dataset is the appropriate evaluation metric. This is important because student inquiry volume would be heavily skewed toward a small number of departments and not evenly distributed. Universities share overlapping jurisdiction and hence the inquiry language can be ambiguous. So, a language model would be ideal for this similar to BERT used in this paper. This paper found that when categories are semantically overlapping, models like BERT get confused. In our case, because of the above mentioned overlapping jurisdiction language, this is highly likely to happen and therefore the current approach of using a large language model (LLM) might be helpful and in my opinion adding a layer of retrieval-augmented + LLM reasoning would be the strongest bet to succeed.
